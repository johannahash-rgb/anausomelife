Communication authorship, FC & RPM||Communication||Communication support should help a person express their own message—not substitute a partner's message.||Facilitated Communication (FC) involves a facilitator's physical support during typing or selection. ASHA describes it as discredited because evidence shows facilitator influence. ASHA does not recommend Rapid Prompting Method (RPM), including related forms, because of prompt dependency and lack of scientific validity. These concerns do not invalidate independently accessed AAC, spelling, eye gaze or typing.||Presuming competence and checking authorship belong together. A person deserves both communication access and protection against someone unknowingly speaking for them.||A team works to establish an accessible selection method in which the communicator, rather than a helper's movement or cues, determines the message.||Ask an AAC-experienced SLP how access, independence and authorship are assessed across partners and settings. Keep other reliable communication available.||How do we know this message belongs to the communicator?||Evidence warning. A dramatic message or a supportive intention does not establish authorship. Do not use unverified messages for high-stakes consent, legal or medical decisions.||fc;rpm||Access method;Presume competence||authorship
