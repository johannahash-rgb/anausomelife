The AUsome edit

Things we would buy again, pack again, wear again — or give without apologizing.

Less scrolling, more useful detail. Our deepest testing lens is profound/high-support autism, but the questions are broader: Is it understandable? comfortable? age-respectful? worth the money? easier on the household? Those questions travel well across the neurodivergent world.

Language note: we use “profound autism” for our own family experience, while recognizing the term is contested and that no single label or family speaks for the spectrum.

Shopping guides

Start with the categories that matter most.

These guides are being built as living resources, not “50 things from Amazon” pages.

The family wardrobe

Polos, oxford shirts, sweaters, practical pants, outerwear, swim, shoes and bags — plus adaptive pieces that do not advertise themselves as adaptive from across the room.

Gifts worth giving

Host gifts, grandparents, teachers, teams, therapists, family friends and the useful little luxuries we would actually be pleased to receive.

The older-teen edit

Age-respectful sensory, AAC-adjacent, adaptive and low-complexity products for a teenager who needs significant support but is still, very much, a teenager.

Weekends away

Luggage, travel organization, tech, hotel must-haves and New England stays where the pool, room layout and practical access details actually matter.

Beautiful + useful home

Bedding, organizing, cleaning, kitchen and family-command-center products that reduce steps without making the house look like a utility closet.

Food made easier

Prepared meals, low-prep kits, bakery and pantry finds, and participation cooking where pour, stir, sprinkle or assemble can count as a real role.

Tech that earns its keep

Smart displays, headphones, AI gadgets, charging, safety and family tech judged by setup burden, subscriptions and whether anybody still uses it a month later.

New England favorites

Local makers, seaside staples, classic regional brands and places worth building a Saturday around.

Our standard

We are picky in useful ways.

For ordinary lifestyle products, we care about quality, price, design, wash/wear, ease and whether the thing becomes part of life. For autism-related products, we add motor and language demand, sensory payoff, frustration, safety, caregiver workload and age presentation.

If we would not tell a friend to spend her own money on it, it does not belong in a “best” list just because it arrived free.

Brands: want to be considered?

We welcome products that genuinely fit one of these guides, especially ongoing wardrobe, gifting, home, travel and higher-value product-testing partnerships.
