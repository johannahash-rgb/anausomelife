A spring visit to Berlin Farms

A little
Easter color.

An egg hunt, a bunny with a few good poses, and a family photograph worth keeping.

An AUsome Life · Published October 7, 2026
From an earlier Easter visit

Pastel eggs across the lawn. White fences beyond the buildings. One orange jacket that had apparently declined the pastel dress code.

Our Easter photographs from Berlin Farms deserved a little space in the journal.

The family storyMap & visit detailsAccess & sensory notesMore photographs

Opened from the family album

The eggs were everywhere.

There is a particular sort of New England spring photograph: green beginning to return to the grass, bare trees in the distance, and everybody sensibly still wearing a jacket. Our Berlin Farms pictures have all of that, with the addition of a lawn covered in pink, yellow, blue and green eggs.

A canvas shopping bag is ready for the egg hunt. The Easter Bunny has a pink bow, a floral backdrop, and considerably more enthusiasm for posing than most adults can sustain. I especially like the photograph with the arms up. There is plenty happening in it, and that smile is the part I keep coming back to.

Then there is the family picture: the three of us together, the bunny at one end, and the stone patio beneath our feet. I am glad we have that one.

Between the photographs

A little room to look around.

The farm makes a lovely setting for this chapter. White clapboard buildings sit beside lines of white fencing, with fields stretching beyond them. In one photograph, a horse is grazing in the distance. The colors are mostly green, white and gray, which leaves the orange jacket with very little competition.

I like having a wider view among the close-ups. It brings back the shape of a place: the deck, the fence, the buildings around the egg-hunt lawn. A family album needs a little of the scenery, too.

One for the album

The good part
is right here.

The photographs beside the black door are worth keeping in the set. There is a cap being adjusted, a yellow egg on the grass, and four very large letters spelling LOVE. The farm has helpfully supplied the caption.

A tote bag, a bunny, and a few photographs I am glad we took.

That is what I want to keep from this Easter visit: the color on the lawn, the bunny pictures, and our family together in the middle of it.

Keep the plan small enough to enjoy

Choose a main activity, make the order clear, and leave room for a break. A bunny photograph can be an option on the day. Watching from a little distance is an option, too.

The useful details

Make the Easter plan comfortable.

Ask about the whole route.

Our photos show grass, stone paving and a wooden deck. Ask about the route from event parking to check-in, the egg-hunt area and bunny photographs, including steps, slopes and any firm, step-free alternative. Wet-weather arrangements are worth checking before setting out.

Check the facilities for that session.

Ask which restrooms will be open, whether there is an accessible toilet, and what changing space is available if your family needs it. Check for somewhere to sit and an easy route back to the car.

Preview the bunny; keep a choice.

A photo of the costume can help make the visit more familiar beforehand. Let each person choose whether to join a photograph, watch, wave or move on. Keep an iPad or other communication support within reach, with an easy way to ask for a break or say “finished.”

Ask about crowds, sound and breaks.

Find out which session tends to have fewer visitors, whether there will be music or amplified announcements, and where someone can step away. Check re-entry arrangements. Those details are useful to ask about directly when deciding whether the event fits your family.

These are planning questions based on the outing and the outdoor surfaces visible in our photographs. Event access arrangements and sensory provisions should be confirmed with Berlin Farms.

Before you leave

The small, sensible list.

  • Confirm the event, tickets, age arrangements and weather plan.
  • Check event parking, the route across the grounds and the open restrooms.
  • Ask whether to bring an egg-hunt bag or basket.
  • Bring a layer, footwear suited to the conditions, and familiar communication or sensory supports.
  • Keep a simple break plan and a clear way to finish the outing.
