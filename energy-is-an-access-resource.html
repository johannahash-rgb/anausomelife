UNIVERSAL DESIGN · FIELD NOTE

Energy is an access resource.

Sometimes access is an elevator. Sometimes it is a seat, a shortcut, a familiar meal, a shorter visit — or deciding that the expensive ride is worth it.

Anything that materially makes participation possible or more sustainable.

Formal access still matters
ADA routes, elevators, seating, bathrooms, communication access and trained staff are not optional extras.

The goal
Participation with enough person left at the end of it.

Access does not begin and end at the ramp.

A place can technically be accessible and still ask an extraordinary amount from the person trying to use it. A long queue can be step-free. A museum can have an elevator and nowhere useful to sit. A hotel can have an accessible room and a breakfast setup that turns the morning into work.

We still care deeply about formal accessibility. We also pay attention to the less glamorous pieces that determine whether access can actually be used: distance, waiting, seating, food, noise, transitions, staff knowledge and how easy it is to change course.

Sometimes universal design is an elevator. Sometimes it’s a pedicab and a good song.

Preserving energy is not cheating.

Take the closer parking spot. Sit down before anybody “needs” to. Skip the second attraction. Buy the familiar food. Ask the employee instead of spending an hour trying to decode the building yourself.

The goal is not to prove that your family can complete the plan in the conventional way. The goal is to have enough access to the actual reason you came.

A pause can be part of the attraction.
Small outings count, too.

Questions we now ask automatically

Where can we sit?

Not “is there seating somewhere?” Where is it relative to the hardest part?

How do we leave?

An exit plan can be the thing that makes trying possible.

Who actually knows?

Find the employee who works the route, desk, entrance or building every day.

Can this be smaller?

One gallery, one ride, one swim or one meal may be the right size.

What is familiar?

A familiar food, object, communication system or routine can carry a surprising amount of the day.

What matters most?

Spend the energy there first.
