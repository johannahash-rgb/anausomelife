Make it · 15–30 minutes

Good photographs deserve a life off the phone.

Make a small, personal gift from photographs you already own: a six-photo envelope, a folded memory card or a ready-to-frame print. Keep the people and the moment exactly as they were.

Choose six photographs, then stop.

Open one occasion or one useful theme: a favorite place, ordinary days together, or a year of small highlights. Choose six photographs that mean something to the recipient. They do not have to tell a whole life story. A cup on a familiar kitchen table or the view from a regular walk can belong alongside a portrait.

Use the original files where you can, rather than screenshots or copies saved from a message. Keep an untouched original. This guide calls for arranging and printing your pictures, without changing faces, bodies, expressions or what happened. If a print format cuts someone off, choose a border or a different size.

Before printing or giving photographs of other people, consider whether they are comfortable with that use. Keep intimate care, distressing moments, private paperwork, addresses and location clues out of the gift. Ask before uploading somebody else's photographs to a printing service or putting them in a shared online album.

Gift 1: the six-photo envelope.

Gather: six 4 × 6-inch prints, one envelope at least 4½ × 6½ inches, one sheet of paper, a pen and optional ribbon. Reuse a clean, sound envelope if it fits without bending the prints. Check dimensions before buying; sizes described as “photo envelopes” vary.

  1. Put the photographs in an order that feels good to look through. Try wide view, detail, people, small moment, favorite, closing image.
  2. Cut or fold the paper into six small caption slips. Write one true sentence per photograph. Avoid dates you cannot verify.
  3. Number the slips lightly and place each behind its matching print. Keep ink and adhesive away from the image surfaces.
  4. Add a note on top: “Six little moments I wanted you to have.” Use your own words if another line sounds more like you.
  5. Slide the stack into the envelope. Tie ribbon around the envelope, not tightly around the photographs.

Have ten minutes? Choose three photos and one note. No printer? Use existing duplicates you are happy to give away, or order prints and give the finished gift when they are ready. An ordinary card today is better than promising a delivery date you cannot control.

A person who wants to join in could choose between two real photos, select the order, pick the envelope or dictate the note using their usual communication. Their decision should appear in the finished gift.

Gift 2: a folded “remember this?” card.

Gather: one sheet of letter-size cardstock or firm paper, one printed photo smaller than the folded card, four photo corners or suitable photo-safe mounting materials, a pen, and an envelope that fits.

  1. Fold the 8½ × 11-inch sheet in half to make a 5½ × 8½-inch card. Firmly smooth the crease.
  2. Place the photo on the front with a comfortable border. Dry-fit everything before attaching it.
  3. Secure it with photo corners, following the packaging. Keep the original unmarked if it is a print you want to preserve.
  4. Inside, finish two sentences: “I remember…” and “I loved…”. Be specific: a funny detail, a place, a kindness. Do not invent a story to make the note longer.
  5. Write your name and, if useful, the date you made the card. Let ink dry before closing it.

For a no-adhesive version, keep the photograph loose inside the card. Choose a larger, easier-to-open envelope if opening small tabs would get in the way. Skip glitter, scents and tiny loose decorations unless they are genuinely welcome.

Gift 3: one print, ready for a shelf.

Gather: one meaningful photo, a frame you already own or have chosen for the recipient, and a note. Measure the actual visible opening and the mat opening, not only the outside of the frame.

  1. Choose a print size and orientation that fit. Preview every edge before ordering or printing.
  2. Make a plain-paper test at home to check positioning before using photo paper. Use the printer's supported paper type and settings.
  3. Clean the frame according to its instructions, avoiding damage to the print. Let any cleaned surfaces dry fully.
  4. Insert the print without cutting through important details. Check the stand is stable or the hanging method is suitable before calling it ready to display.
  5. Include the note separately: “For your desk, if you would like it there.” Let the recipient choose where it belongs.

Consider a lighter or shatter-resistant option where glass would be unsuitable. Do not make wall mounting an unexpected task for the recipient.
