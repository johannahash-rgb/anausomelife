The twenty-minute version is not the consolation prize.

We have had lovely family days that could fit inside somebody else’s parking validation window. Ice cream in Lexington. A short stop by the water. One promising shelf at the Book Barn. A few minutes watching trout in Freeport.

There is a particular kind of pressure around family outings to extract maximum value from the day. If we drove there, paid to park, bought the ticket or packed the snacks, surely we should stay. Surely we should add lunch. Surely we should see the second building.

Except sometimes the extra hour is the thing that makes the whole outing worse.

“We did enough” is a perfectly good ending to a family day.

Energy is part of the budget.

Money matters. Time matters. So does energy. Walking, waiting, transitions, noise, unfamiliar bathrooms, hunger, weather and simply being around a lot of people all spend from the same account.

When we protect some of that budget, the outing gets to remain a memory instead of becoming an endurance test.

One shelf can be the whole Book Barn plan.
Sometimes the harbor view does the work.

Choose the size of the day

Every version counts. ♡
Twenty minutes

Arrive. Do the one thing. Take a picture if you want one. Leave before the day asks for interest.

One hour

Add sitting, a snack or a little wandering. Stop while people still have something left.

Keep going

Only because it still sounds good — not because an itinerary said you were supposed to.

Our visual plan

ONE GOOD STOP
  1. Choose

    One scoop, one shelf, one view. Show the choice in the person's usual communication.

  2. Arrive

    Find the entrance, the place to pause and the way back before adding anything else.

  3. Enjoy

    Do the part you came for. There is no requirement to fill the afternoon.

  4. Finish

    Head home while the good part is still the part everyone remembers.
