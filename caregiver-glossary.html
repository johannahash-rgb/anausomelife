When the jargon starts flying.

Ask the speaker to move from the label to the real-life detail. You are allowed to understand the meeting you are sitting in.

  • What does that look like for this person?
  • How do you know? What observations or data support it?
  • Is that speech, language, movement, sensory input, health or behavior?
  • Where is that support written in the IEP?
  • What can change in the environment before we ask the person to change?

About this guide. We use identity-first language by default, respect individual preferences, and name significant disabilities and support needs without pity. Linked sources distinguish clinical definitions, U.S. education law, research and disability-led perspectives. Our language & representation approach.

This is an editorial learning resource, not individualized medical or legal advice. IDEA entries summarize the U.S. federal baseline; state law and individual circumstances can add requirements. A glossary cannot diagnose a person or determine their legal rights in a specific case.
