The family wardrobe

Good clothes. Ordinary days. Plenty of repeats.

A comfortable waistband, a useful layer, something ready for the pool. Build around the clothes people actually want to wear, with enough room for their own taste.

In this guideBegin with the week you have.Look closer than the size label.Let laundry have a vote.

Begin with the week you have.

Before making a shopping list, look at seven ordinary days. School, work, errands, dinner, the pool, a muddy walk: each asks something slightly different of a wardrobe. The useful question is how many comfortable outfits need to be ready between laundry days.

Pull out the pieces that get chosen first. Notice their shape, fabric, stretch, color and fastenings. A favorite pair of trousers tells you more than a beautifully arranged wardrobe photograph. Ask the person wearing them what works, using the way they communicate best.

  • Count the outfits that fit now and suit the weather.
  • Make a short list of genuine gaps: an everyday layer, a second comfortable bottom, shoes for wet days.
  • Check the occasional calendar too. A coming celebration may need one extra piece, rather than a whole new outfit.
  • Keep favorites in rotation. A small wardrobe needs enough duplicates to get through the wash.

There is no required number of pieces. The right amount depends on laundry, activities, available storage and how often a change of clothes is needed.

Look closer than the size label.

Start with current measurements and the seller's size chart. Where garment measurements are available, compare them with a similar item that already fits. Check whether a measurement describes the body or the finished clothing; they are different things.

The fitting-room questions

  • Can the wearer sit, bend, reach and move comfortably?
  • How do the collar, cuffs, seams, labels and waistband feel?
  • Does a fastening need more grip, coordination or assistance than expected?
  • Will the coat fit over the layer worn underneath it?
  • Does the person like how it looks, as well as how it feels?

When shopping online, check the return window, return postage and any exclusions before ordering. Keep labels attached during an initial try-on where the policy requires it. Personalization and sale items deserve an extra look at the terms.

Adaptive details can be useful when they solve a particular dressing difficulty. Look at the actual closure and how it is reached, rather than relying on the word “easy.” Include any usual dressing support when judging whether the piece works.

Let laundry have a vote.

Read the care instructions before buying. Ask whether the item fits the household's normal washing and drying routine, whether it needs separate care, and whether that extra step is realistic. A lovely jumper that is always waiting for special attention may get very little wear.

For a small, repeatable wardrobe, begin with two or three colors the wearer already likes. Add a comfortable everyday bottom, several tops and a layer that works with them. Navy, cream and a favorite bright color can be a starting point; personal preference gets the final say.

After a few wears

Keep a simple note: chosen willingly, comfortable through the day, easy enough to dress in, and back in the drawer after washing. Once an item is kept, follow its care label and check fit and feel again after the first wash.

Before buying another version, see which pieces are actually being reached for. The wardrobe plan can change. A good repeat is worth more than a coordinated outfit that never makes it out of the house.
