Lunch, with a little Maine around it.

Kittery Food Mart and Fort McClary: a simple takeout idea, a harbor view and the practical route to check.

Let the surroundings do the work.

A paper bag of lunch, a sweater kept handy, boats beyond the old stonework. There is a pleasing economy to a picnic by the Maine water: the meal can be straightforward while the surroundings do the work.

This pairing starts with a food stop in Kittery and leaves room for Fort McClary. Choose what is available and familiar, check the weather, then decide how much of the fort visit suits the day. The view and the historic structure need not be the same undertaking. The family waterfront photograph is a lovely reminder to leave time simply to look.

Getting there

Fort McClary is on Route 103 in Kittery Point, about 2½ miles from US Route 1 and the Maine Turnpike via the rotary, according to Maine’s official guide. Save the state map before setting out.

The state lists daily opening from 10 a.m. to sunset during the Memorial Day–Columbus Day season, unless otherwise posted. Off-season walk-in access is available during those hours, but facilities close. Check the current page before travelling.

Day-use fees apply in season. Open the current state fee page and bring the payment method it specifies rather than assuming card payment at the gate.

For the food stop, Kittery Food Mart lists 2 Shapleigh Road, Kittery, and 207-439-0666. Preview its menu together and call to confirm current kitchen hours.

Access & facilities

Choose the view and the route separately.

Maine’s access guide describes rough steps and steep paths at the fort and blockhouse, and lists the trails as not accessible. It describes bay views as generally accessible and lists accessible parking, picnic tables and a shelter. Read the restroom caveat in the same guide before relying on it.

Those published descriptions are a starting point. Ask the park about your specific route, mobility equipment and current conditions. In-season contact: 207-439-2845.

Keep the picnic practical.

Bring what the meal requires, including water, napkins and any familiar utensils. Pack out rubbish and keep food within ordinary safe time and temperature limits. Stay back from edges and follow posted restrictions around the historic structures.
