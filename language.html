Editorial standard

Words matter. So does context.

An AUsome Life uses clear, respectful language and avoids turning disability into either tragedy or inspiration content.

Identity-first by default

We usually use “autistic person” rather than “person with autism,” while respecting an individual’s own preference when known.

High-support / profound

We use high-support language when it helps explain access needs. “Profound autism” may appear when discussing the specific high-support experience this publication covers; we do not present the term as universally preferred or as a hierarchy of human value.

No pity framing

Support needs can be significant without making a disabled person a tragedy, lesson or campaign prop.

Age-respectful always

Teenagers remain teenagers. We do not infantilize someone because their communication, learning or daily-living support needs are substantial.

For brands

We will not use cure/fixing language, staged distress, forced participation or intimate private details to make a story more emotional. Product claims must stay within responsible evidence.

A little more life. A little less guesswork.
