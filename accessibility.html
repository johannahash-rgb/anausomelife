Make yourself comfortable

Accessibility & reading tools

Different people read, move, listen and communicate in different ways. Choose what helps you enjoy the site.

Find “Accessibility & reading” just below the navigation, or “Reading tools” in the footer. The tools are optional, and you can reset your choices any time.

Make the page work for you

Text & colors

Enlarge text up to 200%, choose a plain typeface, add space between letters, words and lines, or underline links. Choose light or dark high contrast, warm paper, or the original colors. Photographs and communication pictures keep their colors.

Movement & touch

Reduce animation and pause autoplay media. Your device’s reduced-motion setting is also respected. Make controls larger, or use a reading line that follows your pointer or a tap beside text. A keyboard slider adjusts its position too.

A quieter reading view

Open a simpler view of the page’s text, links and image descriptions. Copy or print the reading text. Forms, planners and other interactive tools remain on the original page; return there to use them or open collapsed sections.

Listen at your pace

In reading view, expand “Listen, copy or print.” Choose a device voice and speed, then select “Read aloud.” You can pause, continue or stop; continuing restarts the current passage. Closing reading view stops speech.

This feature is available only when your browser provides a voice identified as local to your device. If no voice is available, use your device’s spoken-content tools.

Keyboard & finding your place

Use Tab and Shift + Tab to move between controls, Enter or Space to activate buttons, and arrow keys for native choices. Escape closes the tools and returns you to the opening button. Use “Skip to main content” or choose a heading under “Jump to a section.”

Your choices, your device

Settings start off unsaved. “Remember my choices on this device” keeps them in this browser for other pages and visits. Uncheck it or choose “Reset all choices” to remove the saved settings. Leave it off on a shared device.

Reading tools & privacy

Use the access tools you already know

Browser zoom, pinch-to-zoom, screen readers, keyboard navigation, voice control, switch access and your device’s display settings can be used alongside these choices. If this browser cannot open the panel, use browser zoom or reader mode and your device’s accessibility settings.

Optional controls are one part of making this site usable. Some pages, embedded media and interactive tools may still have barriers. We have not completed an independent accessibility audit.

If something gets in your way, email us about access. The page link and a brief description help; sharing your device, browser or assistive technology is optional. You do not need to share a diagnosis or private care information.

The details that make an outing possible

“Accessible” can mean almost nothing until somebody tells you about the hill, the gravel, the elevator, the lighting, the quiet corner, the restroom and the walk from the car.

This is the standard we use when researching calendar events, Favorite Places and practical guides. We look for current official information first, then use public visitor reports as a secondary reality check when they add useful lived detail. We label uncertainty instead of turning it into a promise.

What we check

Eight questions before “accessible.”

Not every venue publishes every answer. An honest “not confirmed” is more useful than a confident guess.

Wheelchair + adaptive stroller

Step-free entrances, ramps, elevators, doorway routes, seating, transfer points, wheelchair or mobility-device rentals and whether the route actually works beyond the lobby.

Terrain + distance

Pavement, packed gravel, loose gravel, grass, roots, slopes, long walks, mud, snow and places where a wheeled mobility device may need more help than a general accessibility label suggests.

Sensory + communication

Noise, lighting, crowd density, smells, startling effects, quiet spaces, sensory kits, visual supports, AAC-friendly participation, flexible movement, re-entry and whether “sensory-friendly” changes the environment or only the schedule.

Blind + low-vision access

Braille, large print, tactile or verbal description, audio description, contrast, wayfinding and lighting changes. We avoid assuming that a wheelchair-accessible route is also an accessible visual experience.

Deaf + hard-of-hearing access

Captions, ASL interpretation, assistive-listening systems, visual announcements, hearing loops/headsets and how far in advance an accommodation must be requested.

Restrooms + practical care

Accessible and family restrooms, changing tables, and an adult-size or height-adjustable changing table only when a venue specifically publishes that information. We never imply that a particular family uses a facility simply because we report that it exists.

Parking + arrival

Accessible spaces, drop-off, distance to the entrance, the best door to use, city parking realities, whether a shuttle runs and what happens after dark.

Weather + escape plan

Outdoor exposure, snow, ice, rain, heat, muddy paths, low light, indoor backup options, break spaces and how easy it is to shorten the visit or leave.

Evidence labels

How strong is the information?

Organizer-confirmedA current event page, ticket page, venue policy or direct accessibility statement. This is our starting point.
Venue access guideA current accessibility, visitor, trail, seating or building guide that adds detail beyond the event listing.
Public visitor reportA review or public Q&A can reveal practical realities—steepness, crowd bottlenecks, muddy ground, stroller frustration—but it is anecdotal and dated. We label it as such and do not let it override newer official information.
Not confirmedWe looked and did not find a reliable current answer. That is a prompt to contact the venue, not permission to guess.

Editorial rules

What we will not do.

  • We do not convert “ADA accessible” into a blanket promise about the whole experience.
  • We do not assume a venue has an adult-size changing table, sensory room, ASL interpreter, elevator or smooth terrain unless we can confirm it.
  • We do not present a visitor review as a verified current venue policy.
  • We date access research because buildings, programs, construction, staffing and weather conditions change.
  • We do not diagnose what a person “should” need. The information is there so each reader can decide what matters for their own visit.

The website itself

Access should apply here, too.

We aim for readable type, strong contrast, keyboard-accessible navigation, useful headings, meaningful link text, alt text for informative images, layouts that work on smaller screens, and controls that do not rely on color alone. Visual planners are editable because one person’s helpful wording or picture can be another person’s clutter.

Our tools use visible words alongside icons, large controls, familiar examples, keyboard access and clear optional sections. Preselected settings make it possible to finish without adjusting every detail. Color choices always have written names; color alone does not carry an instruction.

Our creative standard

Beautiful, useful, respectful.

Our default look is preppy New England: Nantucket light, Vermont warmth, recognizable objects and natural materials. Communication pictures keep a clear subject and a familiar word. Decoration belongs around the task, where it can add pleasure without obscuring meaning.

We create and publish lawful, family-friendly content. Sexual content, graphic violence, hate imagery, exploitation, harassment and deceptive images of real people have no place here. We respect privacy, consent, intellectual property and the dignity of the people we portray. AI-created illustrations are identified as such and are never presented as family photographs or evidence about a real venue.

The picture-card studio currently offers a reviewed starter collection and private, in-tab photo selection. It has no public upload gallery and does not generate new images live. Personal photos are not sent to us or automatically inspected. Use only appropriate pictures you are permitted to use. Any future live image-generation service must include input and output moderation before it is made available.

If a page is difficult to use—or a field note is wrong, stale or missing—please contact us with the page and the detail that needs attention.
