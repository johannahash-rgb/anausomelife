The journal/Family notebook

From the family camera roll · Autumn

Apple picking, without turning it into a production.

An orchard gives you something to do and plenty of permission not to do very much. That is a useful combination.

The photographs I keep coming back to are rarely the most polished ones. They are the pictures where the branches are a little unruly, apples have already made their way to the grass, and the afternoon looks like an afternoon rather than an advertisement for one.

That is what I like about apple picking as a family outing. The activity is obvious. Walk a little. Choose an apple. Stop when you have had enough. Nobody gets a medal for adding a farm store, a hayride, lunch and three decorative pumpkins just because October briefly made us ambitious.

The part worth saving

One seasonal thing can be the whole plan.

When a place already gives the day a shape, I try not to overbuild it. A comfortable pair of shoes, familiar food in the car, an easy way to leave and enough time to notice what is actually happening are more useful than a packed itinerary.

There is also something pleasantly forgiving about an orchard. You do not have to move at one speed. People can look, pick, wander, stand under a tree or decide they are finished. The best version is not the longest version.

What I would keep for next time.

  • Choose one anchor. Apple picking is already an activity. It does not need several supporting acts.
  • Dress for the ground, not the photograph. Orchard grass, dropped fruit and uneven paths are excellent reasons for practical shoes.
  • Keep the exit uncomplicated. Know where the car is, keep the essentials with you and let “we are done” be a complete sentence.
  • Take the picture when the moment is there. A real photograph of the day is more useful to us than manufacturing a perfect one.

That is really the family-notebook version of the Good New England Life: not squeezing every possible thing out of a season, but noticing the good thing that is already happening.
