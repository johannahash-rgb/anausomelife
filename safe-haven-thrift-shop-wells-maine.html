Safe Haven · Wells, Maine

A thrift stop for the cats.

Our favorite $1 L.L.Bean find, a vintage Tommy Jeans velour shirt for James’s upcoming school dance, and a fellow thrifter’s directions to the clearance walls. A family stop at Safe Haven in Wells, with practical notes and a free board for a future browse.

October 18, 2026 · From the family camera roll

The little storyThe $1 tipAccess & the upstairs routeFree choice boardPlan a visit

Our favorite find at Safe Haven in Wells was L.L.Bean for $1. We also met a lovely, inveterate thrifter who recommended a vintage Tommy Jeans velour shirt for James, with his upcoming school dance in mind. Retro cool was the idea.

He told us Safe Haven was the absolute best store he goes to, then pointed out the $1 clearance walls along the side and back of the store.

That is the sort of recommendation I like: directions to the dollar section and an eye for a school-dance shirt.

Our October camera roll has plenty else to look at. Floral lamps in a sunny window. Wicker chairs. A patchwork quilt draped over a wooden stand. Baskets, cushions, old records and shelves that reward a second look. This is a shop with quite a lot going on.

The antiques continue upstairs. In our photographs, a large lighted Christmas tree stands beside the wooden staircase. The flowers outside are still firmly in October; the tree has started work early.

And then there is the sign on the door: Don’t let the cats out, no matter what they tell you! A very clear instruction, with room for the possibility that the cats have prepared an argument.

The cats are the reason behind the shopping. Safe Haven says proceeds from its Vintage/Thrift/Antique Shop at Cattail Farm help fund its all-volunteer cat rescue, rehabilitation, adoption and care work in York County. A useful place to browse, with a purpose beyond the next set of shelves.

A tip passed along in the shop

Ask about the $1 clearance walls.

A fellow shopper pointed us to the clearance walls along the side and back of the store. Ask which areas are marked $1, then check each item’s label.

For a clothing browse, check size, care instructions, seams and fastenings before deciding. Prices and stock change; not everything in the shop is $1.

The practical part

A little looking, with the route in mind.

The photographs show the browsing spaces and the stairs we used. Here are the details another family may want before deciding how much of the shop to explore.

What we can show you

Antiques upstairs
The sign directs shoppers up a wooden staircase. We have not confirmed a lift or another step-free way to reach that area. Ask what can be browsed on the entrance level if stairs do not work for your visit.
Closely arranged displays
Furniture, baskets and breakable items sit close together in some photographed areas. We have not measured aisle widths or turning space. Ask about the route for a wheelchair, walker or stroller before relying on it.
Arrival and a place to pause
The exterior photographs show asphalt in front of the shop, with merchandise near the entrance. They do not establish a complete step-free route, accessible parking or a usable restroom. Confirm the details you need and choose a place to pause with your group.
Light and the amount to look at
Our photos show sunny windows, overhead lights, a lighted display tree and plenty of merchandise. Sound levels and busy periods were not recorded. A shorter browse and familiar supports can be part of the plan.
Make the browse as small as it needs to be

Choose one thing to start with: books, clothes, lamps or baskets. Show the person the real options and leave time to respond. “Just looking,” “something else,” “no,” “help,” “break” and “all done” belong in the outing, too.

Set the spending limit that works for your household before browsing. If the $1 section is the goal, ask where it is and check the labels. There is no requirement to buy something or visit every floor.

Check the current entrance and visiting arrangements

The October 16 Weekly Sentinel reported recent collision damage to the Cattail Farm porch and part of the building. Our family photographs document our own visit; they do not establish the repair status or every available route. Ask the shop about its current entrance and the areas open to shoppers.

A useful question: “Which entrance should we use, what can we browse without stairs, and is there a restroom and room to turn with our mobility equipment?”

About the cats

Follow the shop’s posted instruction to keep the cats inside. A shopping visit does not promise a cat encounter. Safe Haven’s current adoption information describes arranging visits through its application process; ask the team about the present setup if meeting cats is part of your plan.

What other visitors have said

On Tripadvisor, Russell W’s November 2025 review describes two floors of books, records, clothing and housewares, while noting that more parking would be welcome. NHjo’s May 2025 review highlights the mix of vintage goods and the rescue purpose. These are individual visitors’ impressions; the stock and conditions can change.

For a future browse
