The family notebook · Little Things That Help · No. 01

Sometimes the straw is the whole trick.

A familiar drink, a bendy straw, and one less thing to wrestle with.

Some of our most useful discoveries begin at the kitchen counter, not in an aisle full of clever gadgets. This one involved a bottle of INTENSE® Lowfat Strawberry Milk we picked up at Wegmans, and the simple preference for a straw.

See what we tried ↓

A small domestic discovery

Keep the familiar thing. Change the fiddly part.

The milk was familiar. The straw was familiar. The awkward part was keeping the straw where it belonged while using the bottle. A tiny adjustment to the setup made the idea worth photographing.

Our first kitchen experiment used an opening in the bottle cap to hold a bendy straw. It was a quick test, not a product recommendation or a how-to for cutting drink lids. If we were setting this up for daily use, we would look for a compatible, food-safe straw lid or pour the drink into a clean reusable cup with its own fitted lid. Cut plastic can leave sharp edges or loose pieces.

The real lesson is pleasantly ordinary: sometimes you don't need to change someone's preferred drink, cup, or routine. You just need to find a way to make it easier to use.

The detail worth noticing

Easy to use should also be safe to use.

A good workaround is one you can repeat safely. Check that the lid and straw fit securely, have smooth intact surfaces, and are clean. Discard damaged parts. If a person has swallowing precautions, use the drinking setup recommended by their care team.

The useful part, in full

Three things to remember.

Start with what works.

A familiar flavor and a preferred straw can be more important than buying another new cup.

Choose a secure, smooth lid.

A compatible, food-safe straw top or reusable lidded cup is a better repeatable choice than a hand-cut cap.

Keep milk cold.

Follow the bottle's refrigeration directions, clean reusable parts and never keep a cap with loose or sharp plastic.

For the communication toolkit

A picture for the preference.

Sometimes it helps to separate the choices: the drink, and the way someone wants to drink it. We sketched two simple prompts—STRAWBERRY MILK with a little watercolor strawberry beside the bottle, and STRAW—for families who find visual supports useful.

Print them, or use the idea to add a familiar photograph and preferred words to an existing AAC system. A picture of the actual bottle may be easier to recognize than an illustration.

Why we keep these little notes

The package is part of the experience.

For some families, the useful detail isn't just what tastes good. It's how a bottle opens, whether a favorite straw fits, how easily the drink can be offered, and whether someone can ask for it in their own way. Those small details deserve a place in an everyday product conversation.
