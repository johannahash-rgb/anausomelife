Find the notes that fit your place in the day.

A good family day has room for different kinds of company: the person who comes for breakfast, the one who knows the favorite music, the supporter who makes an outing possible. These guides offer practical ways to spend time together and be clear about the help each person has agreed to give.

Role names can help you find a starting point. They do not assign duties by gender, biology or household structure. You may recognize yourself in several guides. People receiving support also have relationships, opinions, skills and contributions of their own.

  • Mothers & maternal roles

    Shared pleasures, a whole-job handoff and time that is not entirely taken up by coordination.

  • Fathers & paternal roles

    Breakfast, practical projects, a twenty-minute ball game and everyday responsibilities carried through.

  • Grandparents

    A comfortable welcome, current interests and small traditions that can change with the person.

  • Adult siblings

    Ordinary sibling time, voluntary support, clear limits and a careful start to future planning.

  • Extended & chosen family

    Useful invitations, specific offers and an easy way to join the day without taking over.

  • Co-parents & partners

    Complete responsibilities, clearer transitions and arrangements that fit one home or two.

  • Paid caregivers & direct support professionals

    A clear start, real choices, professional boundaries and records the next person can use.

  • Respite & support partners

    A prepared first visit, familiar activities, dependable coverage and a clear return.

Start with the person and one possible plan.

Ask what sounds good today. Make the available options understandable, using the person’s familiar communication and support. A person may want company, help with one part, time nearby or space on their own.

The NJC Communication Bill of Rights affirms that people should be addressed directly, have meaningful choices and be able to express refusal. Keep those possibilities visible in the plan. If an option is unavailable, explain that and offer the alternatives that are actually possible.

  1. Choose the good part. A familiar stop, one song, a simple meal or a little time outside.
  2. Make it manageable. Start with the shortest version people would enjoy.
  3. Arrange the support. Confirm who is doing what and what information they need.
  4. Leave room to change. Agree a pause, a shorter route or an ending before you need it.

Match the support to the situation.

A relative dropping off lunch, a sibling visiting and a professional taking over a shift are different arrangements. Being present does not automatically make somebody responsible for transport, personal care, money or supervision.

Before sharing a responsibility, check that the person has agreed to it and is prepared to do it. Keep any required training, permissions and current instructions in place. When the arrangement is unclear, settle it before relying on the help.

Supporters have limits too: time, health, access needs, work and other commitments. A dependable modest offer can be more useful than an ambitious plan that nobody can sustain.

Make the handoff part of the plan.

The caregiver handover covers today’s choices, communication, current instructions, contacts and a clear transfer of responsibility. Use it when support is changing hands. For an ordinary household task, start with the smaller household handoff card.

Protect privacy as you pass information along. Share what the particular supporter needs through the appropriate private channel. A warm public welcome does not require a public account of someone’s care needs.

Choose one thing to try this week.

Pick a guide, choose one small idea with the person and decide when it could fit. Use things already available. Afterwards, ask what to repeat and what to change. If the activity was not wanted, choose something else next time.

For more everyday tools, see multimodal communication, the quiet-break plan and the shorter-visit plan. Keep the person’s current support and safety arrangements with the plan.
