Start with the person.

Ask what sounds good today. Make the available options understandable, using the person’s familiar communication and support. A person may want company, help with one part, time nearby or space on their own.

The NJC Communication Bill of Rights affirms that people should be addressed directly, have meaningful choices and be able to express refusal. Keep those possibilities visible in the plan.

Be clear about the help being offered.

Being present does not automatically make somebody responsible for transport, personal care, money or supervision. Confirm what each supporter has agreed to do, and keep any required training, permissions and current instructions in place.

Match the handoff to the task.

The caregiver handover covers choices, communication, current instructions, contacts and a clear transfer of responsibility. For an ordinary household task, use the smaller household handoff card. Share private information only with the people who need it, through an appropriate private channel.

Choose one thing to try.

Choose one small idea together and decide when it could fit. Afterwards, ask what to repeat and what to change. If the activity was not wanted, choose something else next time. Keep the person’s current support and safety arrangements with the plan.
