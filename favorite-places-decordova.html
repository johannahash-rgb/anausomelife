deCordova Sculpture Park

Lincoln, Massachusetts

Art without needing everyone to stand quietly in a gallery. The sculpture park lets you move, look, sit, skip things and keep the visit yours.

Art with room to move.

deCordova belongs among the outdoor places we keep close to home. Our kind of visit leaves room to look, move, pause and decide together which sculpture gets the next few minutes.

The simple good thing

One outdoor loop, one sculpture worth stopping for, café only if useful, home.

deCordova is good for families who do better when the body can keep moving. It also makes a beautiful ordinary afternoon feel more intentional without the pressure of a major museum day.

Accessibility field guide

What we could verify — and what to ask.

“Accessible” is too vague to plan a real day. These are the concrete details we found. Individual needs differ, and venues change.

Verified / published

  • The Trustees lists multiple accessible parking spaces, wheelchair-accessible café/third-floor bathrooms, accessible picnic tables and one accessible pathway through the property
  • deCordova has limited sensory kits available at admissions/gift shop, and The Trustees recommends reserving when possible
  • The official visit page offers app-based sculpture information and a digital/print scavenger hunt
  • As of this October 2, 2026 check, indoor galleries are closed while the outdoor sculpture park, café and store remain the main visit

Ask before relying on it

  • Best accessible loop for today's conditions
  • How to request a sensory kit
  • Any event/setup changing the normal park route
  • Which bathroom is best for your route—the courtyard bathrooms are not wheelchair accessible

A useful access question

“We’re planning to visit on [date/time]. We need [specific feature/support]. Could you tell us the most direct way to access it, where the closest restroom/break space is, and whether anything scheduled that day will substantially change crowds or the normal route?”
