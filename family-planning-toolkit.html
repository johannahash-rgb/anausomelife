Use it · Copy & print

A short plan you can actually use.

Three fill-in tools for an ordinary week: a leaving-the-house card, a one-good-thing day plan and a household handoff. Fill them here, copy them or print a blank set.

Start with one card for one recurring moment.

Pick the transition that regularly creates the most searching: leaving for the pool, getting out for an appointment, or working out dinner. Write down what would actually make that moment easier. A planning card can hold the short list so nobody has to recreate it each time.

Keep a familiar communication system in place. A website worksheet is not a replacement for someone's communication tools or support plan. Words, an existing symbol system, actual objects or photographs may work better for different people. Use the form that is already meaningful, with a respectful tone and a real way to decline or change the plan.

The tools below run in this page. They do not submit or save your entries to a server, and there is no account. Refreshing or leaving can clear your work. Copy or print anything you want to keep. Leave out names, addresses, personal care details, medical information and other private records, especially on a shared device or a sheet left in public view.

Tool 1: the leaving-the-house card.

Write only the things somebody needs to act on before leaving. “Pool bag by the door” is easier to use than “be organized.” Keep the everyday bag and a small last-minute list separate so you do not repack everything.

Before we leave

Place a paper version where the packing happens. If it needs to be carried, fold it into the bag's dry pouch. Review the list after two or three uses and remove anything that nobody needs to see.

Tool 2: one good thing, with room around it.

Plan the part people are actually looking forward to first. Add the arrival, a break and the way home. Keep optional extras genuinely optional. Example: aquarium visit, familiar lunch, home; a second shop remains a possibility rather than a requirement.

A day that fits

Offer choices that are possible that day. Allow time to respond in the person's usual way. A request for a break or an early finish should have a practical response, rather than appearing on paper without a way to use it.

Tool 3: hand over one complete household job.

A useful handoff says what, when and what “finished” looks like. It reduces the follow-up questions without turning the household into a project-management app. Keep private care handovers in their existing appropriate channels.

One complete job

Agree on the handoff with the person doing the job. A card does not assign responsibility by itself. For shared tasks, separate the useful pieces: one person chooses the meal, another gets the pan, another clears the table, with the ordinary help each person needs.

The ten-minute weekly reset.

  1. Two minutes: check the calendar you already use. Pick the two or three plans that affect meals, transport or packing.
  2. Three minutes: choose a familiar easy meal for the busiest evening and check the ingredients.
  3. Two minutes: replenish one recurring kit, such as the swim bag or gift drawer. Add only what ran out.
  4. Two minutes: agree on one household handoff. Check the person has the time, information and materials.
  5. One minute: remove an outdated note. Decide which one card needs to be visible this week.

Try the smallest version for a week. If the card becomes another thing to maintain, shorten it. A plan earns its place by helping the next ordinary moment go better.

Pair this with the five-, fifteen- and thirty-minute household reset or the ready-to-leave bag.
