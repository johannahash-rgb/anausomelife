Family life · A shared tool for every role

The caregiver handover, made useful

Come put the next few hours on one clear page.

Put today’s plan first.

A useful handover helps the next person act. Begin with today’s date, the agreed time together, what the person is expecting and anything that has changed. Keep the long history in its appropriate record.

Make the plan with the person receiving support, using the communication they prefer and any assistance they need. Explain who is arriving, what is happening and how the visit will finish. Offer choices within the arrangements that are genuinely available.

Before responsibility changes hands

  1. Confirm the incoming person has arrived and has agreed to take over.
  2. Review the important changes and invite questions.
  3. Check they can access the needed supplies, contacts and current instructions.
  4. Confirm what happens if plans change or the return is delayed.

When support needs uninterrupted coverage, do not rely on an unanswered message as a completed handoff.

Show how communication works today.

Have the person’s usual communication tools available and ready to use, including a familiar backup if they have one. Show the incoming supporter where they are kept and explain any practical help needed to use them. Do not replace a familiar system with a new worksheet simply because the worksheet is convenient.

Share the useful basics: how the person makes a choice, asks for help, signals a pause and indicates that they want to finish. If a signal is uncertain, say so. “This sometimes means a break; check with them” is more useful than presenting a guess as a fixed fact.

The NJC Communication Bill of Rights supports meaningful choices, direct communication and access to working communication systems. The practical goal here is that the person can still participate when their usual supporter is away.

Keep current care instructions alongside the short note.

The handover is a summary of arrangements, not a substitute for the person’s current care, medical or emergency plans. Confirm that the incoming supporter is authorized, trained where necessary and able to provide the support being transferred.

Give them access to the actual instructions and essential information they need, through the established private channel. Do not omit a critical safety detail merely to keep the summary to one page. Equally, do not improvise medication directions, eating or drinking instructions, lifting methods or emergency procedures in this template.

If instructions conflict, are out of date or cannot be accessed, resolve the issue with the appropriate person before the affected activity or handoff. Follow the existing emergency arrangements for urgent situations.

The short handover template.

Use these headings in the private record or on the paper system you already use. The blank version is deliberately general. Add only the information appropriate for this recipient and setting; keep completed copies secure.

Date and time
Prepared: ______ · Support begins: ______ · Expected finish: ______
Today’s agreed plan
First: ______ · Then: ______ · The shorter version: ______
The person’s choices
What they have chosen: ______ · What is still open: ______
Communication
Tools and location: ______ · Useful partner guidance: ______ · Backup: ______
Food, drink and ordinary supplies
What is ready: ______ · Where the relevant current instructions are kept: ______
A pause or finish
How to check: ______ · Available place or option: ______
Changes that matter today
Confirmed changes: ______ · Question to resolve before handoff: ______
Current care and emergency instructions
Private location and version/date: ______ · Access checked: ______
Contacts and changes of plan
Agreed contact route: ______ · Backup route: ______ · Return-delay plan: ______
Responsibility accepted
Outgoing supporter: ______ · Incoming supporter: ______ · Time confirmed: ______

A completed handover can contain private information. Do not put it on a public noticeboard, leave it in a shared vehicle or paste it into an open online tool. Use the person’s agreed permissions and the provider’s requirements for storage and sharing.

What a useful note sounds like.

Illustrative example: “Today we agreed on the library and home. The shorter version is returning the books only. The communication bag is beside the coat and has been checked. Please confirm the current return time with me before I leave.”

This example does not contain the safety instructions for a real person. Those still need to be accessible to the person providing support.

Close the handover at the other end.

Before leaving, give a brief factual update: what took place, what changed, anything needing attention and who has agreed to the next step. Complete required records in the proper system. Confirm that the next supporter has accepted responsibility where continuous support is needed.

Revise the standing information when something changes. Remove an outdated copy so it cannot be mistaken for the current plan. For ordinary household jobs without private care details, the household handoff card may be enough.
