Weekends · 20-minute setup

Pack once. Reset after the swim.

A practical bag system for a pool visit or one-night trip, with a per-person packing list, a ten-minute departure check and a five-minute unpacking plan.

Build three small zones, using bags you own.

Set out your everyday tote or backpack, two washable bags and a small pouch. Use one washable bag for clean clothes and one for the wet return trip. A shopping bag can work for the journey home; unpack it promptly and keep all bags away from anyone for whom they pose a suffocation risk. A wet bag contains a mess temporarily. It does not dry the contents.

Keep the dry pouch for the small things: the booking reference, locker instructions, and the items that should remain out of splash range. Choose familiar closures the person using the bag can manage with their usual help. Put the first thing you need at the top.

Do a dry run with the actual packed bag. Can its carrier lift it, get through a doorway and find a towel without emptying everything? Split the load if needed. A beautifully organized bag that is too heavy has missed its job.

The pool list: multiply by the people swimming.

Start with these quantities for each swimmer, then adjust for the venue and their ordinary support needs.

  • 1 swimsuit, checked for fit before the day
  • 1 towel, or confirmation that the venue supplies one
  • 1 complete dry change, including underwear and socks
  • 1 pair of suitable footwear for the changing area and journey
  • 1 water bottle and a familiar snack if the venue allows it
  • Usual swim equipment, such as a familiar pair of goggles, when appropriate

For the shared bag, add the booking details, the locker payment or token required by that specific pool, a bag for wet items, and the everyday communication and support items your group uses. Pack sun protection according to product instructions for outdoor plans. Keep individual care or emergency supplies in their usual safe arrangements, rather than relying on a generic website list.

For one overnight

Add one sleep outfit, the next day's outfit, toiletries in a leak-contained pouch, and the chargers actually needed. Count outfits by use: travel, swim, evening, home. Rewear a suitable layer instead of adding a spare version of everything. Keep one change accessible during the journey rather than beneath the whole suitcase.

If a preferred towel, fabric or familiar object helps the outing work, give it a defined spot. Show the arrangement to the person using it, and leave familiar communication tools available throughout the trip.

The ten-minute departure check.

  1. Minutes 0–2: check the pool itself. Look for closures, a changed session time or booking requirements. For a hotel, confirm whether swimming is possible before check-in and whether the luggage needs to stay elsewhere.
  2. Minutes 2–4: pack the fresh items. Fill water bottles, add permitted food, and move the charged devices or communication equipment into their usual places.
  3. Minutes 4–6: agree on arrival. Identify where to park, which entrance to use, and whether your group needs an accessible or family changing space. Check the actual facilities with the venue.
  4. Minutes 6–8: say what comes next. Use the person's established communication: pool, change, snack, home, for example. Keep a shorter version ready if the session ends early.
  5. Minutes 8–10: check people and responsibilities. Confirm who carries what, who has the keys, and the agreed water-supervision arrangements before anyone enters the pool area.

Water safety comes before packing.

A packed bag, swimming ability and a lifeguard do not remove the need for appropriate active supervision. The American Red Cross recommends designated supervised swimming areas and a designated water watcher in group settings. Follow venue rules and the individual's existing safety plan. Pool toys are not safety equipment.

Five minutes when you get home.

  1. Open the wet bag immediately. Hang or wash towels and swimwear according to their care labels.
  2. Empty and clean food and drink containers. Remove anything perishable.
  3. Return keys, paperwork and everyday communication equipment to their normal places.
  4. Write the one thing that ran out or was missing. Replace that item when practical.
  5. Leave the tote open to dry, then put the empty clean and wet-return bags back inside.

Do not leave a fully packed food bag or damp swim kit in the car as a permanent solution. A bag can be ready by having a home and a short fresh-item list.
