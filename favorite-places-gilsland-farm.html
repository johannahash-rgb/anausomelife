A place field note · Falmouth, Maine

Gilsland Farm

Meadow views, birds and a pause near Portland, with a new trail still ahead.

Come along

A quieter chapter
near Portland.

For Gilsland, we would build the day around looking rather than rushing: a landscape, something moving in the trees, a place to pause. The plan can follow the afternoon.

Looking for practical details?

A nature-stop idea

Start with the visitor center.

This is a useful place to put between bigger Maine stops. The grounds offer a visitor center, picnic options and 2.5 miles of trails, so one short section can be a whole visit.

We would begin at the Visitor Center for the restroom, current closures and the route that makes sense that day. Watching birds can be the activity, without adding a longer walk.

Maine Audubon’s All Persons Trail update schedules an opening during October 19–23, 2026. As of this October 4 check, that is still ahead.

The local details

Before you arrive.

1Visitor Center parkingDrive to the end of Gilsland Farm Road+

At 20 Gilsland Farm Road, continue to the Visitor Center at the end of the driveway. The Summer 2026 center map marks accessible parking beside that building; another parking area lies farther up the driveway. Summer 2026 parking map · Current visit information · Construction update

Construction can reserve spaces with orange cones. The future trail’s two-space specification is separate from the current map symbols.

2Visitor CenterAccessible toilets and current trail directions+

Accessible restrooms are inside the Visitor Center. Its normal hours are 10–5 daily, with listed holiday closures. The main trailhead is immediately outside; ask staff which approaches remain open during construction. Visitor Center hours and closures · Accessible restroom location

Use the current closure calendar before depending on indoor facilities.

3West Meadow trail projectOpening scheduled October 19–23, 2026+

The September update describes an approximately 2,265-foot All Persons Trail plus a 318-foot spur. Planned surfaces are hard-packed stone dust and boardwalk, with three bench stops. Average grade is 4%; the spur reaches 8% for 15 feet. Latest official project specifications

Still under construction at this October 4 check. Confirm opening before planning to use it.

4Native Plants Education CenterA separate public stop along the drive+

The Summer 2026 map identifies the Native Plants Education Center as public and marks nearby accessible parking. It is along the approach road, before the Visitor Center. Current public-building and parking map · Sanctuary visit information

The map’s parking symbol does not establish every entrance or indoor facility. Ask about building hours and the route you need.

The details that make the visit possible

Know before you go.

01Visitor Center & restrooms+

The sanctuary page lists free grounds, a Visitor Center open 10 a.m.–5 p.m. daily and trails open sunrise to sunset. Check listed closures, including October 12, 2026. Accessible restrooms are at the Visitor Center; use them before heading out if that suits the visit.

02All Persons Trail opening+

The project announcement gives October 19–23, 2026 as opening week. The design includes approximately 2,265 feet of trail plus a 318-foot spur, hard-packed stone dust, average grade four percent and average cross slope two percent. The spur has an eight-percent section for fifteen feet; rest stops are included.

The announced parking includes a van-accessible space near the trailhead. Those planned features must not be presented as available before opening confirmation.

03Programs & accommodation requests+

Maine Audubon asks for program accommodation requests at least seven days ahead when possible. Check for school groups or events that may affect the atmosphere.

04Picnic, animals & weather+

The visitor information lists accessible picnic areas and asks visitors not to bring pets; registered service animals are permitted. Bring a layer for wind across the meadows and estuary, and a picnic only if it simplifies the day.
