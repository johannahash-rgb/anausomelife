Days out · A better exit

The shorter-visit plan

Decide the small version of the outing before you arrive. It makes leaving a valid part of the plan instead of something that feels like the day went wrong.

In this guideThe anchorStopping pointsExit routeWhat counts

Name the one thing you came for.

Pick the swim, animal, exhibit, lunch or other part everyone is most interested in. If that happens, the day already has a center.

Choose the natural stopping points.

Examples include after one exhibit, after the pool, after lunch, or after the first thirty minutes. Natural stopping points make it easier to check whether everyone wants more.

Make the exit ordinary.

Know where the nearest exit is, how long it takes to reach the car, whether re-entry is possible, and what comes next. Keep the language matter-of-fact: “We’re finished here. Next is the car.”

Do not grade the visit by duration.

A twenty-minute visit can be exactly the right visit. The useful question is whether the person got to take part in a way that worked for them.
