At home · Little things that earn their place

A camping swing, very much at home.

One Walmart camping chair, a formidable collection of stuffed animals, and a reminder that a useful sensory find doesn't always come from a specialty catalogue.

October 21, 2026 · From the family notebook

We have an older photograph from just after we moved in. The house was still settling into itself, and James's stuffed animals had apparently decided to move in all at once.

Right in the middle of it all was a chair we had found at Walmart, sold for camping. It was not purchased as a piece of specialist sensory equipment. It simply became one of those ordinary things that found a good place in our home.

The swing-style, cozy seat was a favorite. The stuffed animals made the scene even better. There is something satisfying about discovering that an object designed for one purpose can turn out to be useful in another part of family life.

We pay attention to what James actually chooses and enjoys, not only what a product label says it is supposed to do. Sometimes the things that earn their keep are expensive and specialized. Sometimes they are hiding in the camping aisle.

A home doesn't need to look like a catalogue to be full of things that work.

The practical details

Where we found it: Walmart's camping section. This is a personal family find, not a verified current listing for the exact chair.

What to check: Camping chairs vary widely. Check the specific model's intended use, weight limit, assembly, stability, and instructions. A portable camp chair is not automatically a therapeutic swing, and rocking or swinging it beyond its intended use may create a tipping or injury hazard.

Our rule: A product that works for one family or one person may not be suitable for another. Follow the manufacturer guidance, supervise as appropriate, and choose purpose-built equipment where needed.
