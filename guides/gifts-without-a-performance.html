Gifts without a performance

Give and receive thoughtfully when surprise, wrapping, public reactions or a long gift-opening session do not suit everyone.

By An AUsome Life · Editorial guide
In this guide
  1. Start with a real interest
  2. Agree on the wrapping
  3. Let the opening have options
  4. Plan for the first use
  5. Keep the story with its owner

A good present can be received quietly. It can be opened later, examined a little at a time or chosen in advance. The pleasure of giving does not have to depend on a surprised expression at exactly the right moment.

When a household includes people with different communication styles or support needs, it helps to separate the gift from the ceremony around it. Some people enjoy the pile of paper and the audience. Others would rather see what is inside before the gathering starts. Ask about the person, rather than deciding on the basis of their age or diagnosis.

Start with a real interest

An older teen's preferences deserve the same curiosity as anyone else's. What do they choose, return to, notice or ask for? Include the ways they communicate, along with observations made respectfully over time. A favorite subject, color, activity or familiar object can be a better starting point than a retailer's generic category.

If you are buying for someone outside your household, ask a person who knows them and, where possible, ask the recipient directly. “Would a book about this interest be welcome?” is more useful than “What do people like them want?” Avoid making the present a disguised task or a statement about what you think the person ought to improve.

Agree on the wrapping

Wrapping is part of the experience and can be adjusted. A bag, an open box or a lightly covered present may be more manageable than several taped layers. Some recipients love an elaborate parcel. Others want to choose the paper themselves or see the gift before it is wrapped.

Use packaging appropriate to the recipient and supervise small parts as needed. Keep instructions, return information and necessary accessories together so the pleasure of the gift is not followed by a search through discarded paper.

A gift card can also need planning. Check where it works, how it is redeemed and whether using it will require transport, an account or an additional purchase. Something that looks flexible to the giver may still create a complicated job for the recipient.

Let the opening have options

Ask before making gift opening a group event. One person may prefer a single present at a time. Another may want to open things privately with their usual support. There can be several perfectly good versions within one celebration.

Do not insist on a photograph or a public thank-you as the price of receiving. Help a person express appreciation in a way available to them, without putting words or feelings in their mouth. An acknowledgment can be brief, supported and given later.

If a gift is declined or set aside, resist explaining the recipient to the room. Give them space. A person can need time, dislike an item or simply be interested in something else at that moment.

Plan for the first use

Ask before assembling, registering or personalizing a gift. Those steps may make a return harder or create an account the recipient did not want. If setup is welcome, make it a complete offer: opening packaging, following instructions, arranging suitable support and putting the unused pieces away.

Check compatibility before choosing a device or accessory. For an outing gift, think about the journey and the arrangements needed to use it. A smaller present that is immediately enjoyable may be more generous than a large one that comes with work attached.

Keep the story with its owner

A lovely moment can remain private. Get permission before sharing a photograph or anecdote, and be particularly careful with children and moments involving support. The recipient's enjoyment should not become material for a public demonstration of generosity.

Our language and representation standard and family-life guides begin with the whole person. Gift giving belongs there too: a real preference, a considerate presentation and enough room for the person to receive it in their own way.
