New England, at your own pace

Accessible trails across New England.

Boardwalks, woodland paths, marsh views and the practical details that make getting outside a little less complicated.

This is a researched planning guide, not a firsthand accessibility audit. Conditions, facilities and staffing change. We separate a designated accessible segment from the rest of a trail system and leave the important limitations in.

The useful question is not only “Is it accessible?”

Look for surface, slope, width, total return distance, parking-to-trail distance, bathroom availability, seating and the easiest place to turn around.

MassachusettsRhode IslandConnecticutNew HampshireVermontMaine

Some days call for a long walk. Others call for a stretch of boardwalk, somewhere lovely to sit and lunch afterward. Both deserve useful information.

An accessible boardwalk is not necessarily a flat route. A nature center bathroom may not be open whenever the grounds are. An “easy” trail is not automatically usable with a wheelchair.

Massachusetts

Short DCR-listed walks, All Persons Trails and marsh, river and woodland views.

DCR says it is gathering more trail-access information and will post updates on its directory. We summarize details published by DCR and linked park pages; if a slope or facility is not stated, it is marked unreported rather than assumed unavailable. See DCR’s Accessible Trails directory ↗

Natick · Broadmoor Wildlife Sanctuary

Broadmoor All Persons Trail

Broadmoor’s accessible route starts at the nature center and includes a boardwalk through marsh habitat, with places to pause and an optional audio experience.

  • Distance: Mass Audubon currently describes the accessible route differently across its pages: a ¼-mile loop on the access page and a 0.6-mile All Persons Trail on the trail page.
  • Surface: Wide path plus wooden boardwalk.
  • Facilities: Accessible nature center and restrooms are listed; coordinate with building hours.

Worth checking: Ask staff which route and return distance they mean on the day you visit, especially if distance is a deciding factor.

Hadley · Silvio O. Conte National Fish and Wildlife Refuge

Fort River Birding and Nature Trail

A longer outing through grassland, woodland and wetland habitat, with viewing decks and benches that create natural stopping points.

  • Distance: About 1.2 miles.
  • Surface: Stone dust and wooden boardwalk.
  • Winter: Trails are not cleared of snow or ice.

Worth checking: A published map marks a restroom near parking, but confirm current opening and the accessible layout before depending on it.

Kestrel Land Trust trail information ↗

Lincoln · Mass Audubon Drumlin Farm

Drumlin Farm All Persons Trails

Two dedicated accessible routes offer a shorter pondside woodland walk or a longer farmyard experience with animal habitats and barns. Drumlin Farm also provides a step-by-step photographic visit preview and accessible audio and print materials.

  • Ice Pond Trail: 350 feet one way from parking toward the Nature Center; five-foot-wide crushed stone path with a guide rope and tactile and Braille stops.
  • Farmyard Loop: Paved and hard-packed surfaces up to nine feet wide. Mass Audubon’s accessibility page says 0.4 mile, while its trails page says ½ mile round-trip.
  • Facilities: Accessible parking and restrooms are listed; loaner adaptive items include a push wheelchair, audio player and seating cane.

Important slope warning: Although Ice Pond meets published accessibility guidelines, portions can feel steep or strenuous. The Farmyard Loop slopes downhill in places; benches provide rest stops. The rough Bird Hill side path is not part of the accessible route. Confirm the intended route length and current facilities before visiting.

Attleboro · Mass Audubon Oak Knoll

Oak Knoll All Persons Trail

A compact, multisensory route through a meadow and woodland edge to a viewing boardwalk over a freshwater marsh. Raised-print and Braille interpretive stops, tactile exhibits and three seating areas give visitors different ways to take it in.

  • Distance: 728 feet total round-trip.
  • Surface and grade: Firm crushed stone with published incline of 5% or less.
  • Extras: A post-and-rope navigation guide, picnic area and printable or customizable tour booklets.
  • Equipment: Ask in advance about a GRIT Freedom Chair, all-terrain rollator and seat cane.

Worth checking: Oak Knoll lists accessible restrooms and a nature center, but confirm opening hours and equipment arrangements. These All Persons Trail measurements do not describe the sanctuary’s other root-filled and uneven trails.

Mass Audubon: Oak Knoll accessibility ↗

Attleboro · Mass Audubon Attleboro Springs

Reflection All Persons Trail

A woodland and wetland loop with bridges, boardwalks and an observation deck over a vernal pool. A guide rope or handrail extends along the accessible route, with Braille markers, audio interpretation and seating cues.

  • Distance: 0.5-mile loop (not the entire three-mile sanctuary trail network).
  • Surface: Wide, level crushed stone with bridges and boardwalk sections.
  • Access: Free admission; daily trail hours are listed as dawn to dusk.

Worth checking: The sanctuary does not list a restroom among its published facilities. Its parking area is not plowed in winter. Confirm the arrival route and any required bathroom stop before visiting.

Mass Audubon: Reflection Trail description ↗ · Directions and winter parking caution ↗

Concord · Walden Pond State Reservation

House Site Trail

A wooded walk from across the Visitor Center to the site of Thoreau’s cabin. The route pairs a short outing with historic interpretation in Walden Woods.

  • Distance and surface: 1 mile out and back on stone dust; published width is 5–7 feet.
  • Published grades: Typical cross slope under 2% (maximum 5%); typical running slope under 5% (maximum 10%).
  • Facilities: DCR lists an accessible Visitor Center and restrooms. The Visitor Center is listed as open 10 a.m.–4 p.m.
  • Parking: Main lot off Route 126; it can close at capacity. DCR currently lists $8 for Massachusetts plates and $30 for out-of-state plates, with fees waived for vehicles displaying disability plates or placards.

Temporary closure: DCR says the Woods Path and Universal Access Trail will close October 19–November 12, 2026 for improvements. The house site will remain reachable via Pond Path, but DCR does not publish access measurements for that alternate route. Call before relying on it during the closure.

DCR accessible-trails directory ↗ · Walden Pond facilities and parking ↗ · DCR closure advisory ↗

Watertown · Charles River Reservation

Watertown Riverfront Park and Braille Trail

A very short loop through a sensory garden beside the Charles River. Interpretive panels, tactile elements and a guide wire offer several ways to explore the setting.

  • Distance: DCR’s accessible-trails directory lists ¼ mile; its Upper Charles self-guided brochure says 0.2 mile.
  • Surface and sensory features: Stone dust; the garden engages touch, hearing and smell. A guide wire with beads marks English- and Braille-labeled panels and seating.
  • Parking and facilities: DCR lists free parking and restrooms for the wider Upper Charles Reservation, but does not locate them at this trailhead or confirm an accessible space here.

Worth checking: The trail listing does not give a route-specific slope or width. Confirm the approach from parking and restroom location before relying on them.

DCR accessible-trails directory ↗ · DCR self-guided walk brochure ↗ · Upper Charles Reservation parking and facilities ↗

Groton · J. Harry Rich State Forest

John Tinker Trail

A short, flat stone-dust out-and-back through the woods and along the Nashua River, with several planned places to pause.

  • Distance: ¼ mile out and back.
  • Rest stops: A bench at the start, two bench rest areas along the way, and a bench and picnic table at the end.
  • Parking: Unpaved trailhead lot with one designated accessible space; the trailhead is on the north side of Nod Road.
  • Not specified: DCR does not give numeric slope or width measurements, or restroom details, in this listing.

Worth checking: Ask about the current condition of the unpaved lot and the route from the parking space to the trail surface.

DCR accessible-trails directory ↗

Topsfield · Bradley Palmer State Park

Accessible Ipswich Riverside Stroll

A level stone-dust path along the Ipswich River, crossing a boardwalk overlooking the river and a beaver-created pond before reaching a footbridge.

  • Distance: DCR’s trail directory lists ½ mile; its suggested-walk guide describes the footbridge as ½ mile from the start, or about 1 mile out and back.
  • Surface and grade: Stone dust and described by DCR as flat/level; no numeric slope is published.
  • Parking and facilities: DCR’s walk guide starts near the main entrance off Asbury Street. The park page lists restrooms, but does not confirm their accessible layout or current availability.

Stay on the main route: A short branch toward the riverbank has exposed roots that DCR identifies as difficult for wheelchairs and strollers.

DCR accessible-trails directory ↗ · DCR suggested-walk guide ↗ · Bradley Palmer facilities ↗

East Boston · Belle Isle Marsh Reservation

Belle Isle Meadow Loop

A stone-dust loop and boardwalk to salt-marsh habitat, with benches, birding and views of planes arriving at Logan Airport.

  • Distance and surface: 0.6-mile loop; stone dust with boardwalk access.
  • Parking: DCR lists a lot inside the entrance gate; a designated accessible space is not specified on the page.
  • Restrooms: DCR’s trail directory says there are none. The reservation page currently lists porta-potties, including one accessible unit.
  • Not specified: The trail listing does not publish route-specific slope or width measurements.

Check before you go: DCR’s pages conflict on restroom availability. Confirm the accessible porta-potty is present and open, and ask about the parking-to-trail approach.

DCR accessible-trails directory ↗ · Belle Isle parking and restroom listing ↗

Rhode Island

Coastal marshes and a woodland boardwalk.

Bristol · Claire D. McIntosh Wildlife Refuge

Nature Center boardwalk

A hard-packed crushed-stone route and wooden boardwalk through fields, woods and marsh habitat toward Narragansett Bay.

  • Boardwalk: About ¼ mile, plus the approach and return.
  • Facilities: Wheelchair-friendly picnic tables are listed.
  • Bathrooms: Available during Nature Center hours.

Worth checking: Beginning October 19, 2026, the center lists Wednesday–Sunday hours. Do not assume bathroom access just because the grounds are open.

Audubon Society of Rhode Island refuge information ↗

Little Compton · Whitehead Preserve

Dundery Brook Boardwalk

A wooden boardwalk through wetland forest, with occasional benches and a quieter inland feel than a shoreline promenade.

  • Distance: About 0.6 mile one way, roughly 1.2 miles out-and-back plus the approach.
  • Surface: Wooden boardwalk; connecting grassy routes are different and may be wet or muddy.
  • Entrance: Use the parking by the tennis courts on Meetinghouse Lane for the boardwalk.

Worth checking: A restroom at the boardwalk entrance and detailed maximum grades were not confirmed in the operator information reviewed.

The Nature Conservancy trail information ↗

Connecticut

A rail-trail park and a call-first sanctuary option.

Simsbury · Stratton Brook State Park

Stratton Brook State Park Trail

A former railroad corridor through a wooded park, crossing brooks and passing through a covered bridge.

  • Distance: About 1.2 miles point-to-point.
  • Surface: Asphalt, cinder and crushed stone.
  • Facilities: Connecticut lists accessible hiking, restrooms, picnic tables and a picnic shelter.

Worth checking: This is not a 1.2-mile loop or continuous pavement. Confirm which restrooms are open, especially outside the main recreation season.

Connecticut State Parks information ↗

Fairfield · Larsen Sanctuary

Edna Strube Chiboucas Special Use Trail

A mile-long trail designed for wheelchair and stroller access within the Larsen Wildlife Sanctuary.

  • Distance: About 1 mile.
  • Setting: Woodland, ponds, streams and fields.
  • Admission: Connecticut Audubon lists admission as free.

Call first: The visitor page currently carries a warning about trail damage and closures after heavy rain. Ask specifically whether the entire Chiboucas route is open and firm enough for your needs.

Connecticut Audubon visitor information ↗

New Hampshire

Two Nature Conservancy options with very different trail surfaces.

Manchester · Manchester Cedar Swamp Preserve

All Persons Trail

One of the more specifically documented routes in this guide, through hemlock forest, rhododendron growth and cedar-swamp habitat.

  • Distance: 1.2 miles round-trip.
  • Surface: Compacted crushed stone and boardwalk.
  • Width: Generally 6 feet, narrowing to 5 feet on the loop.
  • Grades: Published range of 0–9.5%.
  • Resting: Benches approximately 300–700 feet apart.

Worth knowing: “Accessible” does not mean level here. The published 9.5% maximum grade may matter more than the generous width for some visitors.

The Nature Conservancy preserve information ↗

Madison entrance · Ossipee Pine Barrens

All Persons Trail

A wide route through pitch-pine and scrub-oak woodland to an overlook of the West Branch River and the Ossipee Mountains.

  • Distance: About ¾ mile to the overlook, roughly 1.5 miles round-trip.
  • Surface: Hard-packed sand and gravel.
  • Entrance: Route 41 in Madison.

Worth checking: Restroom availability at this entrance and a detailed numerical slope profile were not confirmed in the operator information reviewed.

The Nature Conservancy preserve information ↗

Vermont

Mountain scenery and one very important slope warning.

Stowe · Barnes Camp

Barnes Camp Boardwalk

A short wetland boardwalk at the base of Mount Mansfield with views toward Smugglers’ Notch.

  • Distance: 660 feet one way, about ¼ mile out-and-back.
  • Surface: Wooden boardwalk.
  • Parking: Barnes Camp’s own lot gives the direct approach.

Keep the routes separate: The 1.5-mile Barnes Camp Loop is a different easy-to-moderate hike with a stream crossing. It is not the wheelchair-accessible boardwalk.

Green Mountain Club visitor information ↗

Killington · River Road entrance

Thundering Falls

Roughly 900 feet of boardwalk followed by a stone-dust path toward the waterfall viewing platform.

  • Distance: About 0.3 mile one way, around 0.6 mile out-and-back.
  • Width: Published widths of roughly 3–5 feet.
  • Maximum grade: Short sections reach 12% in the published assessment.

The 12% grade matters: For some visitors the boardwalk itself may be the better outing, turning around before the climb. Use the River Road accessible parking area rather than the upper entrance.

Maine

Salt-marsh views and an Acadia route where boardwalk width matters.

Wells · Rachel Carson National Wildlife Refuge

Carson Trail

A loop through coastal forest with salt-marsh views, numbered interpretive stops and benches at some stops.

  • Distance: About 1 mile in a loop.
  • Surface: Mostly level gravel and boardwalk.
  • Dogs: Leashed dogs are permitted.

Worth checking: The Carson Trail information reviewed does not confirm a restroom at this trailhead, so plan a bathroom stop before arrival rather than relying on another refuge facility.

U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service trail information ↗

Acadia National Park · Sieur de Monts

Jesup and Hemlock Path Loop

Woodland and wetland scenery using compact trail, wooden boardwalk and wider gravel fire road.

  • Route: NPS describes a 1.5-mile figure-eight.
  • Boardwalk width: About 3 feet.
  • Facilities: Designated accessible parking and a public restroom building with wheelchair stalls are listed at Sieur de Monts.

Width check: NPS specifically warns that the boardwalk may not suit some wheelchairs and mobility scooters. Judge the narrowest part, not only the wider approach.

Before you go

Ask about the exact route.

“Is it accessible?” is rarely enough. Ask what section is open today, how far it is from accessible parking and where the steepest or narrowest part occurs.

Make the bathroom question specific.

Ask whether the accessible toilet is open and whether the layout works for the assistance or equipment required. This guide has not confirmed adult-size changing tables or hoists at these destinations.

Check winter separately.

Open year-round does not mean cleared year-round. Ask whether both the accessible parking route and the trail surface are cleared of snow and ice.

Choose the right-sized outing.

A boardwalk, one overlook or twenty good minutes can be the whole plan. There is no extra credit for completing the longest route.
