A place field note · Lincoln, Massachusetts

Drumlin Farm

Farmyard animals, a favorite wildlife stop and unusually useful tools for planning the route.

Come along

A familiar bit
of countryside.

Drumlin is one of the local places in our family’s orbit. Animals, movement and home still close: a familiar outing can leave you free to repeat the parts that work.

Looking for practical details?

From our family life

Animals, then the route that fits.

When a family needs a reliable local option, novelty is overrated. Drumlin lets you return to an animal or wildlife stop, then choose how much more you want to do.

The Farmyard Loop and Ice Pond Trail give that choice a practical shape. The farmyard brings the animals into the route; the sanctuary describes Ice Pond as a quieter place away from the busier farm.

The local details

Before you arrive.

1Visitor parkingOff South Great Road / Route 117+

Enter at 208 South Great Road and use the visitor parking area before admissions. The official map places parking beside the Ice Pond approach; Mass Audubon lists accessible parking. Visitor directions and facilities · Official All Persons Trail map

The pin marks the arrival lot rather than a specific designated stall. Follow current signs.

2Admissions & Nature CenterOrientation, adaptive items and shop+

The Nature Center is the place to request a push wheelchair, seated cane, hands-free binoculars or audio player. Download the step-by-step visit photo tour before arriving. Accessibility and Social Story

Ask about loan availability; the loan listing does not reserve equipment.

3Ice Pond Trail350-foot woodland approach+

The Ice Pond Trail connects the parking lot to admissions. Its crushed-stone surface is five feet wide, with a guide rope, stop markers and seating cues. Detailed trail access specifications · Official route map

Mass Audubon cautions that some sections can feel steep or strenuous despite meeting ADA grade and surface guidelines.

4Farmyard LoopAnimals, benches and bathroom planning+

The 0.4-mile loop uses a nine-foot-wide paved walkway and hard-packed road. It descends between markers 8 and 10, with benches at steeper sections. The official map marks an accessible restroom beside the Red Barn. Farmyard width, slope and stops · Red Barn restroom on official map

A picnic area follows stop 17. Choose a shorter out-and-back if the return slope is too much.

Published information checked October 4, 2026. The live access page gives 350 feet for Ice Pond and 0.4 mile for Farmyard; the map rounds the combined All Persons Trail to 0.5 mile.

The details that make the visit possible

Know before you go.

01Hours, admission & programs+

The sanctuary page publishes current admission and opening details. Hours are 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Tuesday–Sunday and Monday holidays. Admission is $9 adults, $6 seniors 65+ and $6 children ages 2–12; members enter free. Shop hours are Thursday–Sunday and Monday holidays, 10 a.m.–5 p.m. Check scheduled programs separately.

02All Persons Trails+

Drumlin publishes detailed surface and slope information for the Farmyard Loop and Ice Pond Trail. Use the map notes for the specific verified routes and trail descriptions and digital map for the broader network.

03Adaptive equipment & trail formats+

The loan-equipment list includes hands-free binoculars, audio players, a cane with a seat and a push wheelchair. Print, large-print, Braille/tactile and audio materials are also listed. Ask at the Nature Center about availability and the current process for powered-mobility-device day passes.

04Photo tour, restrooms & busy times+

Open the photo tour and Social Story before leaving. The sanctuary lists accessible parking and universally accessible restrooms. Ask about school-group timing and temporary farmyard closures if those will change your route.

05A checklist to take along+

  • Check weather
