Olive Garden · Dinner & communication

James wanted rigatoni. His parents stand corrected.

An Olive Garden dinner, a new friend, and a lesson in making room for James to order for himself. With a free pasta picture choice board.

October 25, 2026 · An AUsome Life

Get the free pasta choice board ↓

At Olive Garden, we made a picture choice board from the menu, then enlarged the four pasta shapes so James could see his options.

First choice: angel hair.

But, like all of us, James is allowed to change his mind.

We ordered for him. He expressed his frustration. Given the opportunity to communicate his own order, he chose rigatoni—and happily ate it.

The lesson for us: let James show the choice board directly to the server. Give him time. Leave room for an answer we weren’t expecting.

Parents develop habits: anticipating, explaining, answering, helping. And ableism can find its way into those habits, including the assumption that we should speak for someone who communicates differently. Loving our child and advocating for him doesn’t mean we’ve finished learning.

We need help, too. We want James to have accessible ways to express frustration and tell us when we’ve misunderstood. Pictures of pasta shapes give us something concrete to work with. Pictures representing abstract emotions remain much harder for us to make meaningful. We’re still figuring that out together.

Meanwhile, we can take his frustration seriously and give him more room to communicate directly.

There was another lovely part of dinner: in the waiting area, we met a fellow disabled teenager who connected with James over his love of Lilo & Stitch. They looked through his AAC communication device together and got to know each other.

It’s one more happy memory at a restaurant we already love for consistently great food, friendly service, and how quickly meals arrive.

A few practical things we appreciate

  • Join the waitlist online before arriving to reduce the wait. Check in with the host; immediate seating isn’t guaranteed.
  • Ask about a table in the bar area. We’ve enjoyed that option on other visits, though we didn’t use it this time.
  • Pay at the table when you’re ready to leave.
