Olive Garden · Dinner & communication

James wanted rigatoni. His parents stand corrected.

An Olive Garden dinner that left us proud of James—and honest about ourselves.

October 25, 2026 · An AUsome Life

Get the free pasta choice board ↓

James chose angel hair. Then he changed his mind. Like all of us.

We ordered for him, and he let us know he was frustrated. He wanted rigatoni.

He ordered it himself. We were proud of him—and humbled. We should have let him show the choice board directly to the server.

Parenting has taught us to anticipate his needs. Sometimes we answer too soon. We can love James completely and still have ableism to unlearn.

We need help, too. Pictures of pasta are concrete; making pictures of abstract emotions meaningful remains elusive for us. We’re still learning how to support him in expressing frustration.

Earlier, James met a fellow disabled teenager in the waiting area. They connected over Lilo & Stitch and explored his AAC together, getting to know each other.

We love Olive Garden’s consistently great food, quick meals and friendly service. Three things make visits easier:

  • Join the waitlist online before arriving to reduce the wait. Check in with the host; immediate seating isn’t guaranteed.
  • Ask about a table in the bar area. We’ve enjoyed that option on other visits, though we didn’t use it this time.
  • Pay at the table when you’re ready to leave.

Olive Garden’s waitlist instructions · Checked October 25, 2026. Seating availability varies; our food and service comments describe our family’s experience.

The pasta choice board

I use ChatGPT on my phone to make choice boards in real time. We haven’t yet found software for James’s AAC device that offers this. We did it for ice cream at Ranc’s, too.
