Olive Garden · Dinner & communication

James wanted rigatoni. His parents stand corrected.

An Olive Garden dinner that left us proud of James—and honest about ourselves.

October 25, 2026 · An AUsome Life

Get the free pasta choice board ↓

James chose angel hair, then changed his mind. Like all of us.

We ordered for him. He was frustrated. He wanted rigatoni—and ordered it himself.

We were proud of him. We should have let him show the choice board directly to the server.

We can love James completely and still have parenting habits—and ableism—to unlearn.

We need help, too. Pictures of pasta are concrete; making pictures of abstract emotions useful remains elusive for us.

In the waiting area, a fellow disabled teenager connected with James over Lilo & Stitch. They explored his AAC together. A new friend before dinner.

We love the food and quick, friendly service. Three more reasons we return:

  • Join the waitlist online before arriving, then check in with the host.
  • Ask about bar-area tables. We’ve enjoyed them on other visits, though not this time.
  • Pay at the table when you’re ready to leave.

Olive Garden’s waitlist instructions · Checked October 25, 2026. Availability and wait times vary; this is our family’s experience.

The pasta choice board

I use ChatGPT on my phone to make choice boards in real time. We haven’t yet found software for James’s AAC device that offers this. We did it for ice cream at Ranc’s, too.
