Wells, Maine · From the family camera roll

A doughnut stop at Congdon’s.

A sunny October Sunday in Wells, Maine: doughnuts, a quieter outdoor spot, and room for James to dance. Plus a free picture choice board for your next visit.

October 18, 2026 · A family field note

The little storyFree choice boardAccess & sensory notesHours & ordering

We came to Congdon’s for doughnuts on a lovely Maine Sunday morning in mid-October. The outdoor area used by Congdon’s After Dark wasn’t busy, so we chose to eat out there.

James had room to dance and feel the air. There were picnic tables, blue sky, and a little space around us. Doughnuts brought us to the stop; being able to enjoy that October morning made us glad we had stayed outside.

We weren’t there for the evening food trucks. I took these photographs during our morning stop: the enormous yellow sign, the red Congdon’s Too! stand, and James getting on with his doughnut beside his dad. The autumn trees were doing their bit for the pictures.

The cornhole-board sign caught our eye, too. We all love cornhole, so that went straight onto the list for another visit. We didn’t play that morning, but doughnuts and a family game sound like a very good reason to come back.

A bakery stop can also involve quite a few decisions. Which doughnut? Where do we order? Are we sitting here or taking it with us? Those are useful things to make clearer before everyone is standing in front of the choices.

So we made a free doughnut choice board for a future stop, with useful communication options alongside the food pictures. This field note also gathers the arrival details we would want another family to have.

Make the choice a little clearer

A picture. A preference. Room to decide.

Plain, honey dip, chocolate raised, or apple fritter? Open the picture board before you order, check what is available, and offer the choices that work for the person using it.

There is room for “something else,” “no,” “more,” “help,” “break,” and “all done,” too. The board is an extra way to communicate; keep the person’s familiar AAC, gestures and other ways of responding available.

The practical part

The route matters as much as the table.

The outdoor area worked well for our morning stop. These photographs also show some of the route and seating details another family may want to check.

Room to move that morning
It wasn’t busy during our October Sunday morning stop, and James had room to dance and enjoy the air. This describes our visit; in-season evening food-truck service is a different setting. Plan for the weather and ask about conditions during your intended hours.
Ordering, waiting and sensory preferences

Show the choices before entering the queue and allow time to respond without repeating the question. Keep “something else” and “no” available. Match a picture to what is actually in the case before treating it as an available choice.

If useful, bring familiar hearing protection and choose a place to pause before you need it. With two adults, one can collect the order while the other stays with the family member in a place that suits them. There is no requirement to turn a doughnut stop into a sit-down meal.

Congdon’s online-order information lists collection at the end of the main bakery counter or at the drive-through when open. Follow your order’s pickup instructions and collect it before the bakery closes at 2 p.m. Moving a preorder to the food-truck park requires advance agreement and email confirmation from Congdon’s.

Parking, bathrooms and the step-free route

The After Dark FAQ describes designated accessible parking along the building and in the gravel lot, plus indoor bathrooms on the shared property. That page concerns the food-truck park; it does not verify every daytime bakery route or the photographed stand.

We did not document the parking-to-door route, restroom layout, door widths or table clearance. If those details decide the visit, confirm the setup during your planned hours.

A useful question: “We’re coming for the bakery. Which parking space and step-free entrance should we use, and can we reach the ordering counter, a table and the restroom by that route?” Contact details are below.

Food ingredients, vegetarian choices and allergies

Congdon’s says its house doughnuts are fried in lard. A plain doughnut therefore is not a vegetarian option. The bakery FAQ points customers avoiding animal fats toward separately packaged alternatives; confirm the individual product and availability.

For allergies or dietary requirements, check the current ingredient and allergen information and ask about the exact item. A picture, flavor name or packaged alternative should not be treated as an allergen guarantee.

The outdoor area, cornhole and an evening visit

For Congdon’s Too!, check the stand’s own hours and menu; we have not verified them. If ice cream is the plan, confirm service before visiting. The Gifford’s sign in our photographs does not establish availability.

We used the outdoor area during our morning doughnut stop. Congdon’s After Dark runs separately as a seasonal evening food-truck park; its website said closed for the season when checked on October 18, 2026. The bakery’s hours below do not describe evening service or guarantee access to outdoor seating on another day.

The sign we photographed says cornhole boards can be signed out at the bar truck free of charge. We spotted the offer without borrowing boards. Before planning a game, check which dates and hours the equipment is available.

What other visitors mention

At the bakery: an August 2025 Tripadvisor review praised the doughnuts and the moving queue, while noting that some choices sold out. A reviewer writing in April 2026 found ordering ahead useful. If a particular flavor matters, the advance-order option is worth considering.

For a future After Dark evening: an August 2025 visitor review highlighted the variety of trucks as a way for a group to find different things for dinner. That is another visitor’s evening experience; our visit described here was a morning doughnut stop.

The After Dark FAQ identifies 5–7 p.m. as its busiest evening stretch. Check the current season, truck schedule and entertainment before planning a return.
