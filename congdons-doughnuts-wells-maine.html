Wells, Maine · From the family camera roll

A doughnut stop at Congdon’s.

Sunshine, a very yellow sign, and James getting on with the doughnut. A little Maine stop, with the useful details left in.

October 18, 2026 · A family field note

The little storyFree choice boardAccess & sensory notesHours & ordering

Some stops earn their place in the camera roll with a big view. This one has a doughnut and an enormous yellow sign. Both make a good case.

Our Congdon’s photographs are full of October sunshine: the red stand, picnic tables, a blue sky, and James taking a bite while standing beside his dad. It is a small piece of our Maine trip, and exactly the sort of ordinary family moment I want to keep.

The red building in these pictures is marked Congdon’s Too! There are serving windows, a little raised porch, entrance and exit signs, and picnic tables outside. James is there with his doughnut, the autumn trees behind him doing their bit for the photograph.

A bakery stop can also involve quite a few decisions. Which doughnut? Where do we order? Are we sitting here or taking it with us? Those are useful things to make clearer before everyone is standing in front of the choices.

So this field note comes with something to take along: a doughnut choice board, a few communication options, and the arrival details we would want another family to have.

Make the choice a little clearer

A picture. A preference. Room to decide.

Plain, honey dip, chocolate raised, or apple fritter? Open the picture board before you order, check what is available, and offer the choices that work for the person using it.

There is room for “something else,” “no,” “more,” “help,” “break,” and “all done,” too. The board is an extra way to communicate; keep the person’s familiar AAC, gestures and other ways of responding available.

The practical part

The route matters as much as the table.

Here is what our photographs show, plus a few practical questions to ask before visiting.

Ordering, waiting and sensory preferences

Show the choices before entering the queue and allow time to respond without repeating the question. Keep “something else” and “no” available. Match a picture to what is actually in the case before treating it as an available choice.

If useful, bring familiar hearing protection and choose a place to pause before you need it. With two adults, one can collect the order while the other stays with the family member in a place that suits them. There is no requirement to turn a doughnut stop into a sit-down meal.

Congdon’s online-order information lists collection at the end of the main bakery counter or at the drive-through when open. Follow your order’s pickup instructions and collect it before the bakery closes at 2 p.m. Moving a preorder to the food-truck park requires advance agreement and email confirmation from Congdon’s.

Parking, bathrooms and the step-free route

The After Dark FAQ describes designated accessible parking along the building and in the gravel lot, plus indoor bathrooms on the shared property. That page concerns the food-truck park; it does not verify every daytime bakery route or the photographed stand.

We did not document the parking-to-door route, restroom layout, door widths or table clearance. If those details decide the visit, ask which entrance is step-free, where to park for it, and whether there is a suitable restroom and table available during your planned hours.

A useful question: “We’re coming for the bakery. Which parking space and step-free entrance should we use, and can we reach the ordering counter, a table and the restroom by that route?” Contact details are below.

Food ingredients, vegetarian choices and allergies

Congdon’s says its house doughnuts are fried in lard. A plain doughnut therefore is not a vegetarian option. The bakery FAQ points customers avoiding animal fats toward separately packaged alternatives; confirm the individual product and availability.

For allergies or dietary requirements, check the current ingredient and allergen information and ask about the exact item. A picture, flavor name or packaged alternative should not be treated as an allergen guarantee.

The red stand, ice-cream signs and After Dark

Our photographs show the Congdon’s Too! stand. Its separate hours and current menu were not verified. The Gifford’s sign in the pictures does not establish that ice cream was available during our stop or will be available on yours.

Congdon’s After Dark is the seasonal evening food-truck park. Its website said closed for the season when checked on October 18, 2026. The bakery’s hours below should not be applied to the stand or food-truck park.
